Small-print asides

Whispering pulls attention away from the headline.
It signals you didn't finish the thought. If the qualifier matters, it belongs in the statement itself.
It quietly drains hierarchy. When every statement ships with a caption, nothing reads as the headline anymore.

(And yes — you just read these three slides exactly the way your audience would.)

Antipatterns

Text too small

If someone in row 30 can't read it,
it doesn't matter how clever it is.

Can you read this?

Neither can the back row. Dense paragraphs, 11-point body text, and a figure crammed into the corner were bad in 2003 and they are bad now. Generative layouts happily reproduce them because nothing in the prompt said "legible from 20 meters." The old rules never stopped being rules — the new tools just make it easier to violate them at scale.
